Understanding what we carry

Trauma can happen in layers.

Not everything we carry began in one moment. Different experiences can shape how we feel, connect, cope and understand ourselves. Explore the influences below at your own pace.

Our environments, family patterns, relationships, emotional responses and self-beliefs can affect one another. There is no one way to understand these experiences.

You can start with whichever layer feels most relevant to you.

✧Environment

The places we grow up in, ongoing stress, instability, cultural context and access to support can influence how safe we feel and how we respond to uncertainty.

What support may look like: Understanding which environments feel stressful or safe, and exploring practical ways to build steadiness in everyday life.

♧Family & generational patterns

We can learn ways of managing emotions, conflict and connection from the people around us. Some patterns may have helped our families cope, even if they no longer serve us.

What support may look like: Noticing family expectations and inherited coping patterns with curiosity, rather than blame.

♡Relationships

Experiences of care, conflict, rejection or disconnection can shape our trust, boundaries and sense of belonging.

What support may look like: Exploring relationship patterns, communication and what supportive connection could feel like for you.

❋Emotions held in the body

Distressing experiences can be accompanied by tension, alertness, numbness or strong emotions that are difficult to put into words. People experience these responses differently.

What support may look like: Building emotional awareness and, where comfortable, using grounding or calming strategies that fit your needs.

◇Thoughts & self-beliefs

Repeated experiences can influence the stories we tell ourselves, such as feeling we must cope alone or never make mistakes.

What support may look like: Gently examining self-talk and exploring more flexible, compassionate ways of understanding yourself.

These layers are examples of overlapping influences—not fixed trauma stages, a diagnosis, or a required healing sequence. You can start wherever feels appropriate.

Getting started

Begin with a conversation.

You don’t need all the answers before reaching out. Here’s what the first steps can look like.

▣
01 —— REACH OUT

Book a free introductory call

Share what you’re looking for, ask questions and discuss suitability. This is not a counselling session.

What happens on the call?

It’s a free 15-minute conversation to ask questions and discuss whether Clarvia’s services may be a suitable fit. You don’t need to share anything you’re not ready to discuss.

☷
02 —— FIND YOUR PATH

Explore your options together

We can discuss which type of support or appointment may best fit your circumstances and preferences.

What could we explore?

Depending on your needs, we can talk about general counselling, alcohol and other drug support, disability-related wellbeing, or other appropriate services.

✧
03 —— MOVE FORWARD

Support at your pace

If we proceed, we’ll agree on goals, practical strategies and suitable next steps together.

What happens next?

You can choose whether to book a session. We’ll discuss a suitable approach and review goals collaboratively, without pressure to commit.

Common questions

Before you reach out.

Prefer to ask something privately? Email Clarvia.

Do I need to know exactly what I want to discuss?

No. We can begin with what feels important now.

Are sessions available online?

Yes. Sessions are predominantly online across appropriate Australian service areas.

How much do sessions cost?

The introductory call is free; general counselling is $135 AUD for 60 minutes, AOD counselling is $160 AUD, and a four-week personalised support package costs $500 AUD. See booking options for the latest details.

Is what I share confidential?

Privacy is taken seriously, but confidentiality has limits where there is serious risk, a legal requirement or another authorised exception. These limits are discussed during consent.
