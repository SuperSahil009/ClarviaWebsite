Optional, practical practices

Find moments of calm, even when life feels overwhelming.

Mindfulness is a practical way of noticing thoughts, feelings and present-moment experiences with greater awareness. It can be explored gently and adapted to what feels safe, useful and realistic for you.

Mindfulness in everyday life

Awareness without pressure.

Mindfulness does not require you to empty your mind or feel calm all the time. It can begin with briefly noticing a breath, a physical sensation, a thought or what you need in this moment.

Practices are optional and individualised. If a particular exercise does not feel helpful, we can pause, adjust or choose another approach.

Possible practices

Choose what supports you.

Depending on your needs and preferences, sessions may include brief breathing exercises, sensory grounding, guided reflection, present-moment awareness or self-compassion practices.

  • Pause and notice physical or emotional cues.
  • Use grounding to reconnect with the immediate environment.
  • Observe thoughts without needing to act on every one.
  • Practise a kinder, more balanced inner response.
  • Bring mindful attention into manageable daily activities.

Individualised support: Mindfulness is not presented as a guaranteed treatment for trauma, addiction, depression or other health conditions. It may not be suitable in every moment or for every person.

A personal connection

Why mindfulness is part of Clarvia.

Shree's own interest in mindfulness developed through personal experiences with meditation and Art of Living practices. That experience shapes a gentle respect for slowing down, while her professional approach remains practical, collaborative and guided by each person's preferences.

Mindfulness can be included within counselling when useful, but it is never a requirement.
