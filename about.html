The journey

Not one straight path. Many moments of becoming.

You don't have to read a long biography to get to know me. These are a few experiences that continue to shape how I listen and support others.

01 · A new beginning

At 19, I started again.

Moving to Australia meant a new country, new routines and the challenge of finding my feet.

Read this chapter

I experienced loneliness, anxiety and uncertainty while adapting to a life far from what was familiar. It shaped my respect for the courage it takes to ask for support during change.

02 · Finding stillness

Learning to pause changed my perspective.

Mindfulness and reflection became meaningful parts of my personal wellbeing journey.

Read this chapter

Through moments of quiet, meditation and self-reflection, I began paying closer attention to my emotions and ways of coping. These practices inform my approach but are never presented as a universal answer.

03 · Study & curiosity

Understanding people became a calling.

Psychology Honours added research and critical reflection to my professional healthcare experience.

Read this chapter

Studying psychology deepened my appreciation for how thoughts, relationships, experiences and circumstances shape wellbeing. My completed Honours qualification does not make me a registered psychologist.

04 · A space to share

Experience made compassion practical.

More than five years across healthcare, mental health and AOD work showed me the importance of being heard.

Read this chapter

Supporting people through acute care, emotional difficulties and substance-use concerns reminded me that change is personal. I value careful listening, collaborative goals and practical strategies, alongside appropriate referrals when a person's needs go beyond Clarvia's scope.

Why Clarvia exists

Because support should feel human.

I wanted Clarvia to be a place where you can bring your questions, complicated feelings and unfinished thoughts without pressure to have everything figured out.

“Sometimes the first step is not fixing everything. It's being able to speak, reflect and feel understood.”

My approach brings together compassionate conversations, psychology-informed reflection, optional mindfulness and practical strategies — always shaped around individual needs and professional suitability.

Grounded in experience

Personal understanding. Professional boundaries.

Authenticity matters to me, and so does being clear about my training and scope.

  • Completed Psychology Honours studies.
  • More than five years in healthcare, including mental health, acute and emergency settings.
  • Alcohol and other drug support, psychoeducation and group facilitation experience.
  • Experience in disability-related support.
  • Registered with Ahpra as a nurse.

Shree is not a registered psychologist or clinical psychologist. Services are provided only within relevant qualifications, competence and professional scope.
